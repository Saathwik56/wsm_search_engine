Query processing is the mechanism by which search engines interpret and evaluate user queries against an inverted index. In boolean retrieval, users formulate queries using logical operators including AND, OR, and NOT.
The query processor parses the boolean expression and intersects the postings lists of the constituent terms. For an AND operator, the postings intersection algorithm matches common document identifiers across lists in linear time. For an OR operator, the union of document lists is computed.
Phrase queries require verifying that words appear adjacently in the exact sequence specified by the user. By inspecting positional offsets stored in the inverted index, the query processor determines whether consecutive tokens are positioned consecutively in the original document.

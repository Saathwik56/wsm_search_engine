Text normalization is a fundamental preprocessing stage in natural language processing and web search. Normalization transforms raw text into a standard format, bridging the gap between varied user inputs and indexed documents.
Key techniques include case folding, where uppercase characters are transformed into lowercase form to ensure case-insensitive matching. Diacritics and accents are normalized so that international characters align seamlessly.
Stopword removal filters out frequently occurring words such as 'the', 'is', 'at', and 'which', which provide minimal discriminative power. Stemming algorithms, such as the Porter stemmer, reduce morphological variants to their root or stem forms, grouping words like 'retrieval', 'retrieved', and 'retrieves' under a common vocabulary key.

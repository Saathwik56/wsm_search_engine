Wildcard queries permit users to retrieve documents when the exact spelling of a term is uncertain or when multiple morphological variants are sought. A typical wildcard query contains an asterisk wildcard symbol, such as mon*, *mon, or m*n.
To support wildcard processing efficiently without sequentially scanning the entire vocabulary, information retrieval systems construct auxiliary indexes. Two primary structures are the permuterm index and the k-gram index.
In a permuterm index, each vocabulary term is augmented with a special terminal symbol such as a dollar sign, and all cyclic permutations are generated and stored in a dictionary. When a wildcard query is evaluated, the query is rotated to position the asterisk at the right end, converting the wildcard match into an efficient prefix lookup.
Alternatively, a k-gram index decomposes words into sequences of k characters. Query terms are segmented into k-grams, and the postings lists of candidate terms are intersected, followed by post-filtering.
